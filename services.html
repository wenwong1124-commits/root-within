Psychotherapy offers a private, supportive space to explore your thoughts, emotions, relationships, and life experiences at your own pace. Whether you are navigating a specific concern or simply feel that something in your life is not quite right, therapy can offer space for reflection, self-understanding, and meaningful change.

Areas We Can Support

  • Emotional Well-Being

    • Anxiety, excessive worry, panic, and overthinking
    • Low mood and depression
    • Emotional regulation and coping
    • Stress, overwhelm, and burnout

  • Relationships & Self-Understanding

    • Self-esteem and self-confidence
    • Relationship concerns and interpersonal difficulties
    • Boundary setting and communication
    • Personal growth and self-understanding

  • Life Experiences & Transitions

    • Grief, loss, and bereavement
    • Life transitions and adjustment
    • Immigration and cultural adjustment
    • Trauma and difficult life experiences
    • Work-related stress and challenges

  • Additional Areas

    • ADHD and autism-related experiences
    • Women's mental health and life experiences
    • Living with chronic illness
    • Caregiver stress and burnout

Who May Benefit From Individual Psychotherapy?

Psychotherapy may be helpful if you:

  1. 1

    Are navigating changes or challenges that are affecting your well-being

  2. 2

    Feel overwhelmed, stuck, or are struggling with difficult emotions

  3. 3

    Want to better understand your thoughts, feelings, patterns, or reactions

  4. 4

    Want to develop greater self-awareness, confidence, emotional resilience, or healthier ways of coping

You do not need to have everything figured out before beginning therapy. Sometimes, knowing that something isn't quite right is enough reason to reach out.

Grief and Bereavement Counselling

Book an appointment

Grief is the natural response to losing someone or something meaningful to you. While grief is often associated with the death of a loved one, loss can take many forms. It can affect our emotions, thoughts, relationships, sense of identity, and even how we experience our day-to-day lives.

There is no right way to grieve, and there is no fixed timeline for healing. You may experience sadness, anger, guilt, loneliness, relief, confusion, numbness, or a mixture of emotions that seem to change from one day to the next. Grief can also come in waves—some days may feel manageable, while others may feel unexpectedly difficult.

Grief counselling offers a compassionate and supportive space to process your loss, make sense of what you are experiencing, and find ways to adapt to life after loss.

Grief Can Follow Many Kinds of Loss

  • The death of a partner, family member, friend, or pet
  • Sudden or unexpected loss
  • Anticipatory grief when someone you love is living with a serious or life-limiting illness
  • Divorce, separation, or the end of an important relationship
  • Miscarriage, pregnancy loss, or fertility-related loss
  • Loss of health, independence, or physical abilities
  • Loss of a job, career, financial security, or sense of purpose
  • A significant life transition or change in identity
  • Moving away from a community, home, or familiar way of life
  • Losses that may not be openly recognized or understood by others (Disenfranchised Losses)

Some losses are visible to others, while others can feel difficult to explain or even name. Your loss does not have to be understood by everyone else for it to matter.

Who May Benefit From Grief Counselling?

Grief counselling may be helpful if you are:

  1. 1

    Finding it difficult to cope with a loss or adjust to life after it

  2. 2

    Feeling overwhelmed, stuck, numb, disconnected, or unsure how to process what happened

  3. 3

    Finding that grief is affecting your relationships, work, sleep, or daily life

  4. 4

    Struggling to find meaning, direction, or a sense of connection after your loss

  5. 5

    Caring for someone with a serious or life-limiting illness and experiencing anticipatory grief

  6. 6

    Simply looking for a safe and supportive space to talk about what you are carrying

You do not need to wait until your grief feels overwhelming to seek counselling. Support can be helpful at any point in the grieving process.
